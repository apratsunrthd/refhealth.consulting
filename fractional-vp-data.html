Match leadership to the work ahead.

Fractional leadership fits a lean team that can execute but needs senior direction: a board deadline, conflicting financial metrics, an AI investment decision, or a hiring plan.

A full-time executive may be the better choice when you need daily leadership across several teams, continuous executive coverage, or a large delivery organization. Use the comparison below to make the capacity tradeoff visible.

Four decisions to get right.

  • Board and metric governanceAgree definitions, owners, reconciliation, and reporting cutoffs. Use MLR and PMPM where the business model calls for them; CAC/LTV and net revenue retention where they inform growth.
  • Architecture and HDAIOS roadmapSequence the Healthcare Data AI Operating System: governed foundations, model access, accountable workflows, and operating cadence. Orchestrate across labs and providers, or go deep with one provider when policy, integration, commitment, or delivery speed makes that the right path.
  • Team design and technical diligenceDefine roles, assess the current stack and delivery capacity, mentor engineers, and build a hiring plan tied to the roadmap.
  • Cloud and vendor negotiationReview commitments, licensing, workload fit, BAA coverage, and provider-specific retention and training controls. Give procurement and counsel a clear technical brief.

Choose the level of involvement.

  • Strategic Advisory RetainerA biweekly executive review cadence, board preparation, architecture oversight, and hiring or vendor decisions. Your team owns delivery; the advisor helps set priorities and resolve tradeoffs.
  • Embedded Fractional LeadershipHands-on roadmap direction, agreed team management responsibilities, and HDAIOS rollout. Define leadership hours, decision rights, engineering capacity, and the handoff before work begins.

How the comparison works

Full-time cash = base + cash bonus + benefits/payroll taxes + recruiting. Fractional cash = 12 × monthly advisory budget + engineering sprint budget. Equity is shown separately.

Runway = cash remaining after upfront costs ÷ monthly cash burn. Recruiting and all sprints are treated as upfront; salary, bonus, benefits, and advisory are spread over 12 months. Recurring leadership costs begin immediately in this runway model; the timing inputs do not shift costs. Burn stays constant; no revenue, fundraising, or productivity changes are assumed.

First metric timing = days until start + days from start to first metric. This is a planning input, not a delivery promise. The options provide different hours and responsibilities; savings do not establish equivalent capacity or business ROI.
